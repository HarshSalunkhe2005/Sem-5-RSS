Numerical 1

Total delay breakdown

Processing Delay = 1 ms, Queuing Delay = 3 ms, Transmission Delay = 2 ms, Propagation Delay = 4 ms
  • FormulaTotal Delay = Processing + Queuing + Transmission + Propagation
  • SubstituteTotal Delay = 1 + 3 + 2 + 4
    • Total Delay = 10 ms

    Courier analogy: label/check the parcel (processing) → wait with other parcels (queuing) → load onto the truck (transmission) → the truck drives city to city (propagation). Total delivery time is the sum of all four stages.

    Numericals 2–3

    Propagation delay & transmission delay

    Propagation Delay

    Distance = 2000 km, propagation speed = 2×10⁸ m/s

  • FormulaPropagation Delay = Distance / Propagation Speed
  • Convert & substitute= 2,000,000 m / (2×10⁸ m/s)
    • Propagation Delay = 0.01 s = 10 ms
    Transmission Delay

    Packet size = 1000 bytes, bandwidth = 1 Mbps

  • FormulaTransmission Delay = Packet size (bits) / Bandwidth (bps)
  • Convert packet size to bits1000 bytes × 8 = 8000 bits
  • Substitute= 8000 bits / 1,000,000 bps
    • Transmission Delay = 0.008 s = 8 ms

    Numerical 4

    Throughput under packet loss

    100 packets sent, each 1000 bytes, 95 arrive successfully within 1 second, 5 lost
  • Successfully delivered data95 packets × 1000 bytes = 95,000 bytes = 760,000 bits
  • Throughput = delivered data / time760,000 bits / 1 s
    • Throughput = 760,000 bps = 760 Kbps

    Even though the link's bandwidth might be much higher, the 5 lost packets (and their eventual retransmission) mean the effective throughput actually achieved is lower — exactly why throughput and bandwidth aren't the same number.

    Numerical 5

    Packet loss rate — buffer overflow

    Router buffer holds 100 packets; 120 packets arrive at the same time
  • Packets storedFirst 100 are stored (buffer capacity)
  • Packets dropped120 − 100 = 20 dropped (buffer overflow)
  • Loss rate(20 / 120) × 100%
    • Packet loss rate ≈ 16.67%
