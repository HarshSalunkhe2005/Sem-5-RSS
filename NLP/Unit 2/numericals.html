TF-IDF

Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency

8. TF-IDF of "sat" in a 3-document corpus

D1: "the cat sat on the mat" · D2: "the dog sat on the log" · D3: "cats and dogs are pets". Compute TF-IDF("sat", D1) using log base 10.

TF(sat, D1) = 1/6 ≈ 0.167 (D1 has 6 words)
"sat" appears in D1, D2 → DF = 2, N = 3
IDF(sat) = log₁₀(3/2) = log₁₀(1.5) ≈ 0.176
TF-IDF(sat, D1) ≈ 0.167 × 0.176 ≈ 0.0294

9. IDF comparison — common vs. rare word

In a 1,000-document corpus: "the" appears in 950 docs, "blockchain" in 5 docs. Compute both IDFs.

IDF(the) = log₁₀(1000/950) = log₁₀(1.053) ≈ 0.022 (very low)
IDF(blockchain) = log₁₀(1000/5) = log₁₀(200) ≈ 2.301 (much higher)
"blockchain" is far more distinctive/informative — TF-IDF automatically suppresses near-universal words.

Language Modeling

Bigram MLE, Chain Rule & Laplace Smoothing

11. Bigram MLE — P("I enjoy reading books")

Corpus: S1 "<s> I enjoy reading books </s>" · S2 "<s> I enjoy watching movies </s>" · S3 "<s> I enjoy reading news </s>". Using P(wᵢ|wᵢ₋₁)=C(wᵢ₋₁,wᵢ)/C(wᵢ₋₁), compute the sentence probability.

P(I|<s>) = 3/3 = 1.0  ·  P(enjoy|I) = 3/3 = 1.0
P(reading|enjoy) = 2/3 ≈ 0.667 (S1, S3)  ·  P(books|reading) = 1/2 = 0.5 (only S1)
P(I enjoy reading books) = 1.0 × 1.0 × 0.667 × 0.5 = 0.333 (≈ 33.3%)

12. Laplace-smoothed probability of an unseen bigram

Same corpus, vocabulary V = {I, enjoy, reading, watching, books, movies, news} (V=7). Calculate the smoothed P(movies | reading), given this bigram never occurred.

Count(reading, movies) = 0, Count(reading) = 2, V = 7
P(movies|reading) = (0+1)/(2+7) = 1/9 ≈ 0.111 (vs. 0 without smoothing)

13. Chain Rule application

Given P(The)=1.0, P(weather|The)=0.4, P(is|The,weather)=0.9, P(nice|The,weather,is)=0.6 — calculate P(The, weather, is, nice).

P = 1.0 × 0.4 × 0.9 × 0.6 = 0.216 (≈ 21.6%)

14. Add-one smoothing with a trigram-style context

Estimate P(ball | "the red") given count("the red ball")=2, count("the red")=10, vocabulary V=100.

P(ball|"the red") = (2+1)/(10+100) = 3/110 ≈ 0.0273

Naive Bayes

Classification Worked Examples

15. Spam classification — single word

P(Spam)=0.4, P('free'|Spam)=0.6, P('free'|NotSpam)=0.1, P(NotSpam)=0.6. Classify the email "free" using only this word.

Score(Spam) = 0.4 × 0.6 = 0.24
Score(NotSpam) = 0.6 × 0.1 = 0.06
0.24 > 0.06 → classified as SPAM

16. Spam classification with normalized posterior

P(Spam)=0.25, P(NotSpam)=0.75. Email X="claim your reward": P(X|Spam)=0.7, P(X|NotSpam)=0.05. Find the classification and normalized P(Spam|X).

Score(Spam) = 0.25 × 0.7 = 0.175  ·  Score(NotSpam) = 0.75 × 0.05 = 0.0375
0.175 > 0.0375 → classified SPAM
Evidence P(X) = 0.175 + 0.0375 = 0.2125
Posterior P(Spam|X) = 0.175/0.2125 ≈ 0.8235 (82.4%)

17. Full Naive Bayes with Laplace smoothing — "I love this movie"

Training data: "I love this movie"→Positive, "This movie is amazing"→Positive, "I hate this movie"→Negative, "This movie is boring"→Negative. Classify "I love this movie".

Prior: P(Positive)=P(Negative)=2/4=0.5
Positive words total=8, Negative words total=8, V=8 → smoothed denominator = 8+8=16
Smoothed: P(I|Pos)=2/16, P(love|Pos)=2/16, P(this|Pos)=3/16, P(movie|Pos)=3/16
P(I|Neg)=2/16, P(love|Neg)=1/16 (raw count 0, +1 smoothing), P(this|Neg)=3/16, P(movie|Neg)=3/16
Score(Positive) = 0.5 × 2/16 × 2/16 × 3/16 × 3/16 ≈ 0.0000824
Score(Negative) = 0.5 × 2/16 × 1/16 × 3/16 × 3/16 ≈ 0.0000412
Score(Positive) > Score(Negative) → POSITIVE

18. Spam detection — "lottery" with full posterior

P(Spam)=0.3, P(NotSpam)=0.7. Email contains "lottery": P(lottery|Spam)=0.6, P(lottery|NotSpam)=0.02. Find classification and P(Spam|X).

Score(Spam) = 0.3 × 0.6 = 0.18  ·  Score(NotSpam) = 0.7 × 0.02 = 0.014
0.18 > 0.014 → SPAM
P(X) = 0.18 + 0.014 = 0.194 → Posterior P(Spam|X) = 0.18/0.194 ≈ 0.928 (92.8%)
